Drafting the PRD, then I’ll package it for the prototype handoff.# Growth Follow-up Nudges
Product thesis: Pixie should turn every upcoming 1:1 into a higher-value continuation of the last one by surfacing one concrete, unresolved growth follow-up for the manager at the moment they prepare.
Problem statement
Pixie’s retention is low because the product is not yet embedded deeply enough in a recurring behavior that users already do: weekly or biweekly 1:1s. Managers may use Pixie once to summarize a conversation or capture notes, but they do not consistently return because the product does not yet create a clear, compounding reason to come back before every next meeting.
A core failure mode in 1:1s is lack of continuity. Growth-related topics—career goals, skill development, stretch opportunities, blockers, commitments, and coaching advice—are often discussed once and then dropped. This creates a poor employee experience and weakens trust in the value of 1:1s. It also means Pixie misses a natural opportunity to help managers do something meaningfully better the next time they meet.
The opportunity is to make Pixie useful in the narrow but high-frequency moment immediately before the next 1:1: “What is the one growth topic I should follow up on?” If Pixie can reliably answer that question with a specific prompt grounded in prior conversations, it can create a repeatable use case tied to the recurring meeting loop.
Why now
Retention is the top product problem. We need a wedge that drives repeat usage, not another broad feature that adds surface area without changing habits.
We already have the core ingredients. Pixie has access to prior 1:1 notes/transcripts/summaries and can identify themes, commitments, and unresolved topics without requiring a new system of record.
The workflow is naturally recurring. 1:1s happen on a regular cadence, which makes pre-meeting preparation an ideal trigger for habit formation.
The scope is constrained enough for MVP. We do not need to solve full coaching plans or long-term growth tracking; we only need to surface one useful follow-up nudge before the next meeting.
This aligns with our strongest product promise. Pixie should help managers run better conversations, not just document them after the fact.
Target user and core job to be done
Target user: Frontline and mid-level people managers who hold recurring 1:1s and want to be more effective coaches, but have limited prep time and imperfect memory across meetings.

Secondary user: Employees indirectly benefit through more consistent follow-through, but they are not the primary user for MVP.
Core job to be done:
Before my next 1:1, help me quickly remember the most important unresolved growth topic to follow up on so I can have a more useful, continuous conversation without extra prep work.
Key insights and evidence
Low retention is likely a workflow embedding problem, not just a feature awareness problem. Users do not yet have a must-return moment.
Continuity is one of the highest-value, most visibly broken parts of 1:1s. Managers frequently forget prior growth discussions, commitments, and coaching threads.
A single, concrete suggestion is more actionable than a broad summary. Managers preparing for a meeting do not want another wall of text; they want one sharp prompt they can use immediately.
Growth topics are especially valuable to follow up on. They signal care, build trust, and are distinct from generic status updates.
Pre-meeting timing matters. Nudges delivered at the moment of preparation are more likely to be used than passive archival notes.
Starting with manager-side prompting avoids workflow complexity. We can create value without requiring employee input, approvals, or a dedicated growth plan system.
Goals and non-goals
Goals
Increase repeat usage by making Pixie part of the pre-1:1 preparation workflow.
Improve continuity across 1:1s by surfacing unresolved growth-related topics from prior meetings.
Deliver one concrete, low-friction manager prompt that is easy to understand and act on.
Validate that follow-up nudges increase engagement with pre-1:1 workflows and downstream 1:1 quality signals.
Non-goals
Building a full employee development planning product.
Creating a comprehensive memory system for all prior 1:1 topics.
Tracking completion of long-term growth plans across systems.
Sending employee-facing nudges or notifications in MVP.
Auto-generating full 1:1 agendas in MVP.
Solving every unresolved topic type; MVP is focused on growth follow-up only.
In-scope decisions
Detect unresolved growth-related topics from prior 1:1 content.
Surface one recommended follow-up nudge before the next meeting.
Show lightweight rationale/context from prior conversation.
Allow the manager to use, dismiss, or skip the nudge.
Instrument end-to-end usage and outcome signals.
Out-of-scope decisions
Multiple nudges per meeting.
Cross-report prioritization across a manager’s whole team.
Employee-visible action items.
Admin reporting dashboards.
Manual rules builders for HR/ops.
Deep integrations with LMS, HRIS, or performance review systems.
Hypotheses
If Pixie surfaces one high-quality unresolved growth follow-up before each 1:1, managers will be more likely to open Pixie before recurring meetings.
If the nudge is concrete and grounded in prior discussion, managers will perceive Pixie as meaningfully improving meeting quality, not just documenting it.
If managers act on these nudges, employees will experience stronger continuity in coaching conversations, which will improve perceived product value over time.
A narrow “one nudge” experience will outperform a broader summary-based experience on clarity, speed, and adoption.
Even imperfect detection can create value if suggestions are timely, editable, and easy to dismiss.

Solution overview
Build a pre-1:1 growth follow-up nudge module that appears in the manager’s preparation experience for an upcoming recurring 1:1.
For each upcoming 1:1, Pixie will:
Review prior meeting artifacts for that manager-report pair.
Detect whether a growth-related topic was discussed previously and appears unresolved.
Generate one recommended follow-up prompt for the manager.
Present it in a compact card before the meeting with enough context to build trust.
Let the manager take one of several lightweight actions: use it in prep, add it to agenda/notes, dismiss it, or indicate it is no longer relevant.
What the nudge should contain
Prompt: one concrete follow-up question or suggestion the manager can ask.
Topic label: e.g., career growth, skill development, stretch opportunity, blocker, commitment.
Why this is showing: brief reference to the prior discussion.
Source context: snippet/date from the prior 1:1.
Action affordance: add to agenda, copy, dismiss, snooze/skip for this meeting.
Example
Prompt: “Last time, Alex said they wanted more exposure to roadmap work. Check whether you’ve identified a concrete opportunity for them this month.”
Why this is showing: “Growth topic discussed 2 weeks ago; no follow-up detected in later notes.”
Source: “I’d like more chances to work on strategy, not just execution.”

UX principles
One card, one recommendation, one clear action.
Trust over coverage: better to show nothing than a weak suggestion.
Ground every recommendation in a prior conversation artifact.
Keep prep-time overhead under 10 seconds to parse.
Avoid language that sounds robotic, generic, or overly therapeutic.
Edge cases
No prior 1:1 data: do not show a nudge.
Only one prior meeting with sparse notes: only show if confidence remains high.
Topic already resolved in later notes: do not show.
Multiple unresolved topics: choose the best one by recency × importance × actionability; do not show multiple.
Poor or noisy transcript quality: fall back to summaries/notes if available; otherwise suppress.
Long gap since prior discussion: optionally deprioritize stale topics unless explicitly recurring.
Manager changed or employee changed teams: suppress if continuity is unreliable.
Sensitive content misclassified as growth: suppress categories that may feel risky in MVP (e.g., compensation, performance risk, personal/health topics) unless explicitly framed as development and safe to surface.
Manager already added similar agenda item manually: if detectable, suppress duplicate recommendation.
One-off 1:1 or ad hoc meeting: do not trigger unless clearly part of recurring 1:1 workflow.

Risks and open questions
Risks
Low precision erodes trust quickly. A few obviously wrong nudges may cause managers to ignore the feature entirely.
Recommendations may feel generic. If phrasing is not specific and grounded, the feature will be perceived as AI filler.
Coverage may be low. Many 1:1 records may not contain enough structured evidence to generate useful nudges.
Sensitive-topic mishandling. Surfacing the wrong kind of prior topic could feel invasive or unsafe.
Usage may not translate to retention. Managers may like the card without changing their ongoing product habit.
Inference of “resolved” may be weak. We may over-show already addressed topics or miss good follow-ups.
Prep surface discoverability may be insufficient. If users do not open prep consistently, value remains capped.
